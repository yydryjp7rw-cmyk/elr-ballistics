ELR BALLISTICS: PUTTING IT ON YOUR iPHONE SO IT WORKS WITH NO SIGNAL
=====================================================================

Why this needs a one-time web address
-------------------------------------
An iPhone only keeps a web app working offline if it was first opened from a
real web address (https). Opening index.html straight from the Files app won't
run the calculator reliably and won't save it for offline use. So you put
these files on a free website once. After that, the phone keeps its own copy
and runs with no signal.

The files (keep them together, don't rename them)
-------------------------------------------------
  index.html            the calculator
  sw.js                 the part that saves the app on the phone for offline use
  manifest.webmanifest  tells the iPhone the app's name and icon
  icon-180.png, icon-192.png, icon-512.png   home-screen icon

Step 1: Put the files on a free website (GitHub Pages, one time)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Easiest on a computer.
 1. Make a free account at github.com.
 2. Create a new repository named, for example, elr-ballistics. Make it Public.
 3. In the new repository choose "Add file", then "Upload files". Drag in all
    six files above and commit them.
 4. Open the repository's Settings, then Pages. Under "Build and deployment"
    pick "Deploy from a branch", choose the main branch and the / (root)
    folder, and save.
 5. After a minute or two the page shows your address, something like:
      https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/elr-ballistics/
    Open it on a computer to check that the calculator loads.

Any other free host that serves plain files over https works the same way.

Step 2: Install it on the iPhone (needs signal, one time)
---------------------------------------------------------
 1. On the iPhone, open that address in Safari.
 2. Tap the Share button, then "Add to Home Screen", then "Add".
 3. Open the app from the new home-screen icon while you still have signal.
    Wait until the box at the very bottom of the app turns green and says
    "Ready for offline use".

Step 3: Test it before you go off grid
--------------------------------------
 1. Turn on Airplane Mode.
 2. Close the app completely (swipe it away), then open it from the icon.
 3. Pick the .375 CheyTac preset, enter 2200 yards, and press Calculate.
    You should see 53.5 MOA. If you do, it's ready for the field.

Good to know
------------
 - Saved rifles and your last inputs are stored inside the home-screen app.
   They are separate from Safari, so set up your rifles in the app itself.
 - Leave the website up. The phone checks it now and then when it has signal.
   If the site is deleted, the offline copy isn't guaranteed to stay.
 - Keep the spreadsheet or a printed dope card as a backup. Deleting the app,
   or the phone running very low on storage, can remove the offline copy.
 - Updating the app later: upload the new files to the same repository, and
   change the version number in sw.js (elr-ballistics-v2 to v3, and so on). Then open
   the app twice with signal. The first opening downloads the update, and the
   second shows it.
 - A full table to 5 miles in 5-yd steps (1,741 rows) took about 13 seconds on
   the test computer. The app shows its progress while it works.
 - "Share or save CSV" opens the iPhone Share sheet, so you can Save to Files,
   AirDrop, or send the table by Messages or Mail.

Adding a new rifle
------------------
 1. Tap "New rifle" at the top. The rifle and bullet boxes are cleared;
    weather settings are kept.
 2. Fill in muzzle velocity, BC and BC type, bullet weight, diameter and
    length, sight height, zero distance, and twist rate and direction.
    If anything needed is missing, the app tells you which box and takes
    you to it.
 3. Tap "Save rifle" and give it a name. It appears in the list marked
    "(saved)". To change it later, edit the boxes and save again under the
    same name. To discard an unsaved new rifle, tap Delete.

Barrel angle column (dope table)
--------------------------------
Shows how far the bore points above level for each distance, including any
uphill or downhill slope you entered. That's what an inclinometer reads on
a surface parallel to the bore, such as the receiver. A tapered scope rail
reads lower by the rail's built-in MOA (20 MOA = 0.33 degrees, 40 MOA =
0.67 degrees).
